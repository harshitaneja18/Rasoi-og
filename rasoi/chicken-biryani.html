Royal Rice Masterpiece

Chicken Biryani

Layers of long-grain basmati and spice-marinated chicken sealed in a heavy pot and slow-steamed until every grain carries saffron, rose water, and the perfume of whole spices.

1. Mughal Origins

Biryani takes its name from the Persian birian, meaning “fried before cooking”. It flourished in Mughal royal kitchens, where Persian pilaf techniques met Indian spices, and spread across the subcontinent into dozens of regional styles — Lucknowi, Kolkata, Hyderabadi, Malabar, and Ambur among them.

2. The Dum Technique

Marinated chicken and parboiled rice are layered in a handi, sprinkled with saffron milk, ghee, mint, and crisp birista (fried onions), then sealed with dough. Slow heat from above and below lets the meat juices steam upward through the rice, cooking both together.

3. How It Is Served

Biryani is traditionally served with cooling raita, a tangy mirchi ka salan or salan, and sliced onions with lemon. It is the dish of weddings, Eid feasts, and Sunday family lunches.
