Samosa

A flaky, deep-fried pastry pyramid packed with spiced potatoes and peas — India’s best-loved tea-time snack, sold at every railway platform and street corner.

1. A Traveller from Central Asia

The samosa arrived with Central Asian traders as the sambusak, a meat-filled pastry. Medieval Indian chroniclers such as Amir Khusrau and Ibn Battuta described it at royal tables. Over centuries India made it its own with the potato filling that is now standard.

2. The Perfect Crust

The dough is rubbed with generous ghee or oil (moyan) and ajwain, then fried slowly in medium-hot oil. This low-and-slow fry creates the characteristic blistered, crisp, flaky shell.

3. Serving Styles

Samosas are eaten with green chutney and sweet tamarind chutney, or crushed into samosa chaat topped with chole, yogurt, onions, and sev.
