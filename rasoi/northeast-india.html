Northeast India: Ancient Fermentation & Forest Foraging

Shielded by mist-shrouded Himalayan foothills, the eight states of Northeast India preserve a breathtakingly unique culinary universe. Unlike the heavy powdered masalas of the mainland, Northeast food relies on boiling, steaming, smoking, and exquisite microbiology.

1. The Science of Khorisa & Fermentation

Fermentation is the lifeblood of Northeast preservation. Fermented bamboo shoot (known as Khorisa in Assam, Soibum in Manipur, and Bastenga in Nagaland) is prepared by finely grating fresh tender young shoots of wild bamboo and packing them tightly into earthenware jars or bamboo tubes. Natural anaerobic lacto-fermentation transforms the woody fibrous shoots into an umami-rich, sour, intensely aromatic seasoning that pairs magically with pork, fish, and greens.

2. Assamese Khar: Ancient Alkaline Alchemy

An authentic traditional Assamese meal always begins with Khar. Unique across global gastronomy, Khar is an alkaline extract obtained by sun-drying the peels and trunk of wild indigenous bananas (Bhimkol), incinerating them to ashes, and filtering clean water through the ash residue. This naturally alkaline filtrate is cooked with raw papaya, pulses, or river fish, acting as a gentle digestive cleanser that prepares the stomach for subsequent courses.

4. The Ghost Pepper (Bhut Jolokia) & Wild Herbs

Indigenous to Assam, Nagaland, and Manipur, the Bhut Jolokia (King Chilli) was certified by the Guinness World Records as the hottest naturally occurring chilli in the world. Rather than using it in powdered form, Northeastern cooks gently dip a single fresh pod into simmering broths for just 30 to 60 seconds, or mash it with roasted tomatoes and fermented fish (Ngari) into aromatic relishes.
