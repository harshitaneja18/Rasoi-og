Quick Spice Facts

Botanical Name Syzygium aromaticum

Key Bioactive Compound Eugenol (70–90%)
Aroma Profile Pungent, sweet, medicinal, numbing warmth
The Piercing Bud

Cloves (Laung)

Shaped like tiny antique nails—from which its Latin and Hindi names originate—the clove is the unopened flower bud of a tropical evergreen tree. In Indian cuisine, a mere two or three buds suffice to perfume an entire cauldron of rich gravy or spiced biryani rice.

Intense & Numbing Flower Bud Garam Masala Anchor

1. Aroma Chemistry & Eugenol Power

Cloves possess the highest concentration of essential oil of any dried spice (up to 20% by weight). Its volatile profile is overwhelmingly dominated by eugenol, an aromatic phenylpropanoid that produces an intense, sweet-spicy sensation followed by a characteristic pleasant numbing effect on the oral mucosa.

2. The Smoke Technique (Dhungar)

In royal Awadhi and Mughlai kitchens, cloves play a theatrical role in the ancient smoking technique known as Dhungar. Red-hot wood charcoal is nestled in a small metal cup atop cooked meat or dal. A couple of crushed cloves and a spoonful of ghee are dropped onto the coal; it erupts in dense, sweet clove smoke, and the pot is immediately sealed to infuse deep, campfire-aromatic layers.

3. Traditional Medicine & Oral Health

Time-Honored Toothache Remedy

Every Indian grandmother knows to hold a whole clove against an aching tooth. Modern dentistry confirms eugenol possesses strong antiseptic, antibacterial, and mild anesthetic properties, making it the active ingredient in traditional dental cements and pain-relieving tinctures.
